Geometki is a small hobby project. I have been building it on my own, in my spare time, since 2013. It is not a company or a startup: there is no marketing team and no investors behind the site, just one person who loves exploring interesting places and writing code.

A geotag is a mark on a map with exact coordinates: latitude and longitude. Travellers use such marks to pin a place they have found, to come back to it or to show others the way. Hence the name: the whole site is a collection of such marks.

## How it started

The project was first called Green Expedition and lived at greenexp.ru. It was an open encyclopedia of interesting places: anyone could put a waterfall, a cave, an old manor or a spring on the map, upload photos and the GPS track of their hike. Today greenexp.ru leads here: Geometki is the same site, grown up.

Over the years the project has been rewritten many times, almost from scratch. Partly because that makes it better, partly because it is the best way for me to keep learning programming: every new version is a chance to try new technologies on something real.

## Two hobbies in one

Geometki brings together two of my passions. The first is programming. The second is exploring: hiking, camping, trips to places no guided tour will take you. An abandoned manor in the next district, a spring in the woods, a hill none of your friends has climbed — they all turn out to be close by once you know where to look.

The site grew out of one question: where else could we go?

## Why

I build the project for everyone, and I want people to explore their own region more: to get out of town, to go hiking on their own, without tour agencies, to move and to spend their weekends outdoors rather than in front of a screen.

The world we live in is beautiful and unique. There are thousands of interesting places around us, each with a character of its own. Climbing a mountain and seeing the world from above is one feeling. Stepping into an abandoned manor or an old factory, where every wall keeps the work and the daily life of people who lived there before us, is quite another. And monuments let you touch the events and the people of years gone by. All of it is close at hand — you only need to step outside.

A geotag on the map is a hint of where to go. Not a guidebook with a ready-made route, but a mark: there is something interesting here, this is what it looks like, this is what is known about it. The rest is your own adventure.

## The game

Geometki recently got a game. Now you can compete with other explorers: visit a place, check in with the "I was here" button or a photo, and earn geopoints. The harder a place is to reach, the more geopoints it gives, and the first ones of the season get a bonus. Seasons change with the seasons of the year, and each starts from zero, so you can always catch up with the leaders.

The game is just a reason to finally get to the place you have been meaning to visit. More on the ["How to play"](/en/game/help) page.

## A map made by everyone

The world is huge, and no one can travel all of it alone. The map has long had places beyond Russia, and there are more of them every day. Almost every place on the site was added by visitors — people who have been there. So Geometki lives as long as people use it. Here is how you can help:

- **add a place** you know, even if it seems nobody would care about it;
- **upload photos** of the places you have been to: fresh pictures show what a place looks like today;
- **check in** when you visit a place and answer a few short questions about it, so others know what it is like now;
- **rate and comment** on places and **fix the descriptions** when something has changed;
- **tell your friends** you go hiking with about the site.

## Gently

Many places on the map are fragile: old buildings, springs, protected corners of nature. Nature and people shaped them over decades and centuries, and they can be destroyed in a minute.

We are the keepers of this heritage. Our shared mission is to hand this fragile world on to the next generation as wonderful as we found it. That takes nothing special: only respect for the place and for those who will come here after us.

Everything that depends on us is simple. Leave no litter and take your rubbish with you. Break nothing, take nothing away, leave no writing on the walls. Light no fires where they are not allowed. Explore — and preserve: capture the place, the nature, the moment in a photo, and leave them as you found them. The best trace you can leave is a photo on the site, not a mark on a wall.
